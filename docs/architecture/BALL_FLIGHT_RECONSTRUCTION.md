# Estimated physical ball flight and reference evaluation

Implemented 8 October 2026 as an isolated statistical/geometry module around the three vision models. The production pipeline's physical ball speed remains unavailable. No independently measured real tennis speed reference has passed qualification.

`src/analytics/flight_reconstruction.py` accepts a metric pinhole camera with separately supplied intrinsics, a proper world-to-camera 3D rotation, translation in metres, calibration provenance and undistorted observed pixels. The world frame uses metres and +z upward, with the court on z=0. A 3x3 image-to-ground homography is insufficient and is rejected. Intrinsic/extrinsic matrices must be independently calibrated in the actual camera session; a synthetic known camera is permitted only for software tests.

Observed samples have stable IDs, actual source seconds, image coordinates, camera ID and optional boundary-before marker. Original timestamps must be strictly increasing. Predictions/interpolation are not observed reference positions. `split_flight_observations` splits at racket contacts, bounces, cuts, camera changes, unobserved samples and time gaps. `reconstruct_flight_segment` accepts exactly one impact-free observed segment. Its default numerical scope is at least six observed samples, at least 0.10 seconds, at most 2.5 seconds and no gap over 0.10 seconds. These limits and residual/conditioning tolerances are algorithm starting settings, not independently qualified video limits.

The motion model is r(t)=r0+v0*t+g*t²/2 with g=(0,0,-9.80665) m/s². Projective image equations form a linear system for six initial-position/velocity unknowns. Time scaling and normalized projection constraints reduce unit-dependent conditioning. SVD rank/conditioning, positive camera depth, pixel reprojection, nonnegative ball height, bounded apex height and physical plausibility gates withhold inconsistent estimates. Evidenced racket-contact/ball-centre bounce heights can constrain segment endpoints. Endpoint times must exactly match source observations; a known bounce may additionally provide independent court x/y position. No contact height or landing position is invented from an airborne ground homography.

Deterministic ±0.5-pixel stress patterns test depth and speed/vector sensitivity. Excessive sensitivity withholds the estimate. This is a numerical diagnostic, not a confidence interval, measured detector uncertainty or physical accuracy. Fit residual alone cannot qualify reconstruction. Drag, spin, wind, rolling and impacts within a segment are neglected; real tennis may violate these assumptions. Timing, camera calibration and independent physical reference errors are not converted into an invented interval.

The output is always `estimated_unqualified` or `unavailable`, with a reason, source observation IDs, sample/time window and diagnostics. Successful outputs contain estimated 3D positions and initial velocity. `segment_average_physical_speed_kmh` integrates the travelled ballistic arc length and divides by the observed segment duration; it is distinct from endpoint displacement/time or image-plane speed. `racket_impact_speed_kmh` is supplied only at an evidenced racket contact at a segment endpoint. `impact_speed_side` identifies outgoing speed at the start or incoming speed at the end; it is not automatically a serve-speed measurement. `uncertainty_interval` is null, and `qualification_status` records the absence of an independent synchronized reference. Future production integration must preserve these states until the relevant gates pass.

The [primary Where Is The Ball paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05763v1) identifies monocular 3D ambiguity and sensitivity of physics methods to projectile segmentation and tracking noise. Its learned reconstruction method and real-tennis results do not establish project ball-speed accuracy. This implementation uses the physical/projective formulation without importing its additional neural networks, which would violate the current three-model boundary.

Run the independent reference harness once permitted synchronized measurements exist:

```powershell
python scripts/evaluate/evaluate_ball_flight.py --manifest data/ball_speed_references.json --output artifacts/validation/ball_speed_reference_report.json
```

The JSON input requires `schema_version="1.0"`, `usage_rights="authorized_for_evaluation"`, `all_strokes_enumerated=true`, a declared data kind and a `strokes` array. Each stroke has a unique ID, match ID, stroke type, metric camera, observed samples and optional evidenced start/end boundaries. Keep occluded/unmeasurable strokes in the array with an unavailable reason. References require `status="independently_measured"`, source, synchronization evidence and speed in km/h. The definition is either `segment_average_physical_speed` with exactly matching source start/end seconds, or `racket_impact_speed` with exactly matching source contact timestamp and incoming/outgoing side. A radar contact measurement cannot substitute for a segment average. These declarations require an external audit of the source and actual synchronization, rather than becoming independent evidence merely by being present in JSON.

The harness reports every stroke, estimate coverage, reference coverage and unknown counts separately per stroke type and measurement definition. Matched references produce median/P95 absolute error, MAE and bias. The proposed user gates remain median absolute error at most 5 km/h and P95 at most 10 km/h. A sparse or synthetic sample cannot certify those targets. Reports retain manifest/implementation SHA256 hashes and remain unqualified pending independent reference and sample review, even if synthetic numerical errors pass. No physical speed is promoted to production.

Software evidence: `artifacts/validation/yolo26_execution_2026-10-08/flight_reconstruction_evaluation.xml` records 19 synthetic algorithm/harness tests passed in 0.42 seconds. They recover known-camera ballistic trajectories, compare travelled-speed integration with an independent dense integral, distinguish contact speed, reject invalid depth/rank/cameras/timing/impacts and verify measurement-window/coverage controls. They are numerical correctness checks, not detection, speed or real-match accuracy tests. The first local run had one test fail because subtracting large PTS values made exactly 0.10 seconds slightly smaller in floating point; a 1 ns numerical boundary tolerance corrected that issue, without relaxing the physical/timing qualification gates.
