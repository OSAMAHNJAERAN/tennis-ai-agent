# Tennis analytics evidence for the YOLO26 execution brief

Checked 8 October 2026 (Asia/Singapore). Primary-source research and planning recommendations only; no training, data acquisition, deployment, or promotion of current qualification status. Model-version evidence is researched separately. Proposed numerical targets below are project targets, not published YOLO26 capabilities or achieved results.

## Geometry and physical measurement

OpenCV defines a homography as a mapping between planes and describes robust estimation from point correspondences. Court line intersections with fixed metric coordinates can therefore calibrate the court plane. Use redundant, distributed intersections, independent withheld checks, distortion handling when available, and validity checks after camera pan/zoom/cuts. Four clicks are a mathematical minimum rather than an accuracy certificate. [OpenCV homography theory](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d9/dab/tutorial_homography.html), [OpenCV robust estimation](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d1/de0/tutorial_py_feature_homography.html).

Planning inference: player movement can use observed foot/ground-contact locations mapped to the court plane and original presentation timestamps. A bounding-box bottom centre is an approximation; foot occlusion, jumping, box jitter, and smoothing bias must be evaluated. Airborne ball pixels projected by the same ground-plane transform are not their true ground projections or 3D positions. Two known bounce positions and elapsed time supply a horizontal displacement/time summary, not instantaneous flight speed or racket-impact serve speed. Do not label it as either.

The CVPR workshop paper *Where Is The Ball* explicitly identifies ambiguity in reconstructing 3D from a 2D trajectory and states that its real tennis data lack 3D ground truth. Its tennis landing result is not independent physical ball-speed or close-line validation. Require calibrated physical reconstruction plus synchronized reference measurements before publishing physical ball speed: radar with its measurement definition, or independently calibrated high-speed/multiview measurement with uncertainty. Reprojection consistency alone cannot certify physical accuracy. [Author paper, introduction and experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05763v1), [CVF publication](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/CVSPORTS/papers/Ponglertnapakorn_Where_Is_The_Ball_3D_Ball_Trajectory_Estimation_From_2D_CVPRW_2025_paper.pdf).

## Rules, contact and coloured court zones

The official 2026 rules specify 23.77 m court length, 8.23 m singles width, 10.97 m doubles width, and service lines 6.40 m from the net. Measurements include the outside of lines; touching a line counts in. A serve must land in the diagonal service court. First and second service faults, service lets, double bounces, game/set/match scoring, service rotation, and change of ends are distinct state transitions. Alternative formats need explicit configuration. [ITF Rules of Tennis 2026, rules 1, 5–7, 10, 12, 17–24 and Appendix VI](https://www.itftennis.com/media/7221/2026-rules-of-tennis-english.pdf).

Planning inference: detect a physical bounce before highlighting a service box or tactical zone. Colour its complete calibrated polygon in the source view and synchronized mini-court, with translucent fill and a temporary contact marker. A valid bounce does not automatically award a point. Use the active point state, service number, side, latest hitter, and terminal event. Do not use a ball centre inside a polygon as a substitute for a line-contact footprint. Include localization/calibration/contact-time uncertainty; classify as `in`, `out`, or `uncertain`, and abstain when uncertainty crosses the relevant line. Wide broadcast footage often cannot establish close line calls, foot faults, net contact, or causal forced/unforced-error judgments.

## Training data and validation independence

TrackNet is primary evidence that broadcast tennis balls are small, blurry, sometimes occluded, and benefit from temporal context. Its original paper reports F1 of 98.5% on a particular final and 84.3% in its additional-video cross-validation. These figures are neither YOLO26 guarantees nor end-to-end scoring accuracy; the domain difference motivates strict match/session-level separation. [TrackNet paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03698).

Phase 1 recommendations: permission-cleared professional broadcast-style elevated camera behind one baseline; court and two singles participants visible; preserve original resolution, frame rate and timestamps. Exclude spectator handheld, unusual sidelines, edits, replay duplicates, and close-ups from supported analysis. Curate varied matches, venues, court surfaces, lighting, player clothing and ball sizes within that supported viewpoint. Freeze train/validation/calibration/final-test manifests by complete match/session, prevent duplicate/near-duplicate clips across splits, and keep final labels model-blind. Do not promote existing development clips to an untouched test set.

TrackNet's original dataset link was inaccessible in this bounded check; no raw-media commercial licence was verified. A public video or code licence does not itself establish permission for broadcast acquisition, redistribution, or model training. Retain source, permission, licence, permitted uses and immutable revision per asset.

CalTennis is a 2026 tennis human-motion and multiview benchmark, captured with synchronized cameras at 60 Hz; it is not an automatic source of measured ball trajectories or event labels. The publisher dataset listing declares CC BY-NC 4.0. It can inform a separate research validation track when use rights, viewpoint, camera mappings, synchronization and ground truth are independently verified; it should not silently broaden phase-1 training views or qualify a commercial public deployment. [Author project](https://ilonadem.github.io/caltennis-website/), [author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20542), [publisher dataset listing](https://huggingface.co/datasets/demalenk/caltennis/tree/main/02_24_2026_16_30_court1). Preserve the unresolved issues in the [existing camera audit](CALTENNIS_CAMERA_CONVENTIONS.md) and [7 October data recheck](ACCURACY_DATA_RECHECK_2026-10-07.md).

## Recommended three-model boundary

The selected project plan uses only three primary trainable YOLO26 vision checkpoints: (1) a specialist YOLO26m tiny-ball detection checkpoint with native-resolution crops/tiles; (2) a YOLO26s-pose participant checkpoint producing both bounding boxes and 17 anatomical keypoints; (3) a separate YOLO26s-pose court checkpoint trained with a documented custom 14-intersection schema. The participant pose checkpoint already provides boxes, so a fourth player detector is unnecessary. Verify official task/configuration/export support and freeze the resulting checkpoints before release. Court intersections and human anatomical points are different schemas and checkpoints.

Tracking association, player identity, motion constraints, calibration, bounce/hit inference, rules, statistical score-and-serve probability propagation and overlays are pipeline modules rather than additional mandatory deep architectures. Improve data/crops/resolution and temporal association within the selected family without a mandatory model-family comparison. Sizes are initial engineering selections that must meet frozen accuracy/latency gates; do not promise suitability before measurements. This is a project choice, not a scientific assertion that YOLO alone solves events or monocular physics.

## Evaluation and release gates

HOTA was developed to evaluate detection, association and localization together; use it with IDF1, identity-switch counts, participant recall, and fragmentation to inspect tennis identity continuity. Per-frame detection mAP alone cannot establish stable tracking. [HOTA paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07736).

Project target recommendations, to freeze or revise with stated justification after baseline annotation and pilot measurements:

| Component | Required measurements and provisional target |
| --- | --- |
| Players | IDF1 >= 0.95 in supported live-play segments; publish HOTA, per-match switches and far/near-player recall separately. |
| Ball observations | Visible-ball precision/recall >= 0.95 under a frozen centre-distance criterion; report median/P95 pixel error at native resolution, normalised error, blur/occlusion subsets, gap lengths and visible observation coverage. Inferred/interpolated locations never count as observed detections. |
| Court/player position | Independently labelled reference error in metres, median <= 0.20 m and P95 <= 0.50 m for player ground locations; do not use fitted line residual alone as validation. |
| Events | Hit/bounce precision >= 0.95, recall >= 0.90 within an explicit time tolerance (initially +/-80 ms); one-to-one matching, event-type confusion, false events per play minute, negative warm-up/dead-ball controls. |
| Player speed | Independent reference MAE <= 0.5 m/s on supported intervals; report bias, P95 error, reference uncertainty, coverage and measurement window. |
| Physical ball speed | Median absolute error <= 5 km/h and P95 <= 10 km/h on separately measured supported shots, if attainable; otherwise expose unavailable values and record the failed gate. Separate impact speed from segment-average speed. |
| Point outcomes | >= 0.95 precision on automatically decided point winners plus a coverage floor frozen before final evaluation (initial proposal >= 0.80 of supported evaluable points). Publish unresolved points and whole-match scoring drift; a low-coverage accuracy claim cannot qualify the product. |

Line-call qualification needs a separate close-line benchmark with independently measured contact/line positions and uncertainty; coarse 0.2–0.5 m localization gates cannot qualify centimetre claims. Build rule-engine tests for all supported formats; test whole matches and partial clips starting mid-game. Allow manual initial score, player identities, server and scoring format, with optional scoreboard OCR as corroborating evidence. Without adequate initial/final state or independent confirmation, report clip point outcomes and an unknown actual match winner. Enforce explicitly labelled observed/inferred/manual/unknown provenance, coverage and reasons for abstention in both dashboard and report.

Live and offline evaluation must be separate: offline smoothing may use future frames; live inference may only use frames already received or a declared bounded look-ahead. Benchmark capture-to-dashboard P50/P95 latency, sustained source-rate throughput with all enabled stages, actual hardware/configuration, dropped frames, queued workloads and long-session stability. Do not transfer another developer's GPU or clip throughput claim to this product.

## Match-win probability and explanations

Calibration means predicted probabilities match empirical outcome frequency; reliability plots complement Brier loss and log loss, which also measure discrimination. Fit calibration on independent data. [scikit-learn probability-calibration documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html), [Guo et al., On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599).

Planning inference: start with a tennis score/serve-state probability model using shrinkage-estimated serve-point probabilities and rule-based game/set/match propagation. This keeps the three-checkpoint vision budget. For every time t, use only score, completed points and observations available through t; forbid final score, future frames, later commentary and whole-match aggregate leakage. Split training/calibration/test by match and chronology, account for repeated snapshots from one match, publish reliability and Brier/log loss against a score-only baseline, and distinguish forecast probability from a confirmed result. No guaranteed future winner. Explain point loss with evidence and timestamps (for example double fault, confirmed out, net, or unreturned ball); tactical explanations are hypotheses with supporting measurements rather than invented causes.
