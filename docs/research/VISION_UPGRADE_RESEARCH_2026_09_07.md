## Reference architecture, verified from code

| Cells | Component | Actual behavior |
|---|---|---|
| 2, 4 | Video and model setup | Reads `data/clip.mp4`; obtains dimensions/FPS through OpenCV. Width and FPS scale thresholds from a 640-pixel, 25-FPS baseline. Loads the CourtSide community detector's `tennis_ball` class and `yolo11n-seg` for people. Device selection is MPS or CPU; CUDA is not selected. |
| 6, 8 | Court calibration | Contrast ridge mask, Hough segments, orientation clustering, cross-ratio candidate sets and court-template coverage. Every twentieth frame contributes to a median consensus. An unregistered temporal median of up to 60 images becomes a clean plate. Powell optimization fits four corners against nine painted court lines. |
| 10, 12 | Court coordinates and players | Computes homography and eight court regions. Recovers camera pose assuming square pixels and a centered principal point. Uses bbox bottom-center for ground position and bbox top for height. Selects at most one person per net side using height, court proximity and previous position. |
| 18, 19 | Audio and cached Pass A | Audio becomes mono 22,050-Hz STFT spectral flux in a 1,500–8,000-Hz band. Pass A runs ball detection at image size 1280/confidence .20, person segmentation at 960/.35, and court fitting using calibration and previous-fit seeds. Pickle caches contain candidates, masks, boxes and court data. |
| 21 | Ball association | Removes spatially recurring candidates using whole-clip KD-tree counts. Interpolates rejected court corners and applies a centered median. Tracks one `(x,y,vx,vy)` Kalman state, selects one candidate using one-row Hungarian assignment, applies uncertainty gating, coasts through gaps, trims trailing coasts and backward-smooths with RTS. |
| 23, 24 | BallNet recovery | Small encoder–decoder with skip connections, channels 16/32/64 and two pooling levels. Input is **three grayscale frames**, not three RGB frames. Trains on 128×128 crops labeled by the first tracker. Searches track-derived corridors on initially unseen frames, appends accepted candidates at fixed confidence .35 and reruns association. |
| 25 | Events and 3D | Combines image-y reversals, audio peaks, silhouette proximity and horizontal reversal. Heuristics add or relabel bounces. Court zones may snap an outside bounce to a nearby zone. Contact heights and gravity supply modeled ball height. There is no dedicated validated serve/shot-speed estimator. |
| 14, 27–34 | Visualization and exports | Draws minimap, trajectory, person boxes, court lines and zone flashes. Exports scene JSON and an offline canvas viewer. Pass B renders H.264 without inference; its FFmpeg command does not mux the source soundtrack. |

### Limits and failure paths that matter for reuse

These are code-review findings at the fixed revision, not failures measured by running new videos.

- **Restricted viewpoints:** cell 6 `detect_court()` assumes near-to-far ordering by image-y and rejects a far baseline width at least 98% of the near width. It uses 35°/145° orientation boundaries, a minimum projected depth, constrained area and at least 75% of template samples on screen. Side-on, overhead, rotated and tightly cropped footage can violate these tests.
- **Incomplete camera-cut handling:** cell 8 combines sampled views into one consensus and unregistered clean plate. Cell 19's shot score remains tied to the original calibration. Cell 21 rejects large departures from calibration and interpolates across the whole clip. A cut between two wide court shots need not reset the track.
- **Single-player-per-side prior:** cell 12 `players_in_world()` is for singles. It favors estimated height near 1.8 m and treats bbox bottom-center as a foot contact. This is neither a persistent identity tracker nor measured pose. Jumping, crouching, occlusion, wheelchair players and children challenge those assumptions.
- **Racket proxy:** cell 25 `racket_point()` projects a point 0.9 m above player ground position. It does not detect a racket. `racket_gap()` is defined but unused; primary classification uses silhouette proximity and reversal heuristics.
- **Modeled height:** cell 16 fixes racket-contact height at .9 m, maximum height at 2 m, maximum anchored-arc duration at 1.2 s and one-sided extrapolation at .4 s. These limits are not suitable evidence for arbitrary serves, overheads and lobs.
- **Mixed camera geometry:** cell 25 `fit_arc()` uses global `CAM_XY/CAM_Z`, although other stages carry per-frame camera pose.
- **Outside-zone snapping:** cell 10 `zone_at_snapped()` and cell 16 `ZONE_SNAP_M=1.5` can light an inside region for a bounce outside it. That display behavior must not become an in/out or target-success label.
- **Event consistency is not truth:** cell 25 assumes a hit should generally have a preceding bounce, relabels the first of adjacent same-side hits, and reports same-kind neighbors as missing events. Volleys and rally boundaries invalidate a strict hit–bounce alternation rule.
- **Possible `KeyError`:** cell 25 lines 527–531 creates an impulse event with `source="modelled"` when the ball was not seen, without a `world` key. Lines 541–542 access `ev["world"]` for every modeled event.
- **Broken unavailable-camera fallback:** cell 10 allows `CAM_Z=None`, but cell 12 formats it as a floating-point value. Cell 32 also calls `S.camera.z.toFixed(1)` without a null guard.
- **Gap-sensitive derivatives:** cell 25 computes `_vel_all` over compressed nonmissing positions at uniform sample spacing, collapsing gaps and potentially crossing segments.
- **Cache provenance omissions:** cell 19 keys data by file size, integer mtime and selected ball settings. Checkpoint hashes, segmentation/court settings and BallNet training settings are absent. Changing them can reuse stale inference or weights.

### What the reference actually evaluates

Cell 23 uses the first tracker to create BallNet pseudo-labels. Its positive coordinates are RTS-smoothed positions from whole-clip tracking. Static negatives also depend on the whole clip. Validation holds out temporal blocks 7 and 8 out of ten, with no guard interval: adjacent three-frame windows can share imagery across the boundary. Two jittered crops per accepted frame create correlated examples, and difficult frames the teacher did not accept are omitted from positive validation.

The accept test **skips negative crops**, so it does not measure false positives or precision. A detection means the heatmap maximum exceeds .30. Localization compares to pseudo-label peaks using `px(4)` tolerance. The printed PASS condition is only 60% successful positive-crop localization. Weights are saved before that check; FAIL does not disable inference, and cached weights bypass validation. NumPy randomness is seeded, but PyTorch model/training randomness is not.

Other runtime diagnostics count candidates, fitted courts, tracked/coasted frames, events and modeled heights; court fit errors reuse the paint-distance objective. They do not provide independently labeled ball precision/recall, player AP/IDF1, bounce accuracy or speed error. README manual-check and simulation claims have no corresponding reproducible evaluation assets in this checkout.

## Single-view geometry and speed

A court homography relates points on a plane. It does not measure the unknown height of an airborne ball. Applying it to the ball image center gives a camera-ray intersection with the court, and differentiating that location does not yield true ball speed. This follows from the planar geometry documented by [OpenCV](https://docs.opencv.org/4.5.1/d9/dab/tutorial_homography.html). Monocular trajectory research adds learned or physical constraints; it does not validate this project's estimates automatically. See [Where Is The Ball: 3D Ball Trajectory Estimation From 2D Monocular Tracking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05763).

Engineering recommendation: expose observed court-contact coordinates separately from modeled airborne coordinates. Report calibrated player ground speed only when foot localization, court calibration and timestamps are valid. Withhold absolute airborne/serve speed until validated against synchronized independent measurements; label constrained 3D reconstructions as modeled and retain uncertainty. Camera pose, lens distortion, timestamp quality, contact timing, drag/spin and out-of-plane motion are material error sources. A smooth line or low reprojection residual is not a speed-accuracy measurement.

## Proposed benchmark and selection procedure

1. Freeze baseline commit, weights, checksums, preprocessing, tracker settings and environment. Store raw predictions separately from rendering.
2. Build match-disjoint evaluation clips covering professional/amateur play, near/far players, camera angle, resolution, lighting, court surface, motion blur, net/player occlusion, scene cuts, out-of-frame ball and false-positive distractors. Do not randomly split neighboring frames. Track provenance and label quality.
3. Start with the installed detector, a resolution/crop ablation, WASB tennis and one temporal alternative. Expand only when results justify the extra work. Use validation matches for thresholds, then freeze them before the held-out comparison.
4. Evaluate raw detections and completed trajectories separately. Define visibility, localization tolerance and matching rules before measuring. A wrong-location prediction on a visible-ball frame contributes both an unmatched prediction and an unmatched target; interpolation is not detector recall.
5. Report ball precision/recall/F1, localization errors, false alarms per minute, track fragmentation, lost duration and recovery delay. Include per-match and condition-stratified results, sample counts and uncertainty. Preserve observed, predicted and missing states.
6. Compare player trackers with identical cached detections. Measure detection AP/IoU, IDF1/identity switches, occlusion recovery, and near/far pose joint error. Measure racket detection and keypoint accuracy rather than visual appeal alone.
7. Enable event and speed acceptance gates only after tracking prerequisites pass. Measure bounce/contact timing and location against independent labels. Validate speed with a separate trustworthy measurement source, not a model-generated pseudo-label.
8. Measure end-to-end throughput, p50/p95 latency, initialization time and peak memory on actual deployment hardware. Record input resolution and temporal look-ahead; offline smoothing is not a live result.
9. Select a candidate only after comparable measurements show an improvement with acceptable costs. Training, if needed, should use reviewed labels, match-disjoint splits, consistent geometric/temporal augmentations and checkpoint provenance. Synthetic tests check mechanisms; they cannot establish real-match generalization.

There are **no measured project before/after performance values in this report**. The requested greater-than-95% ball precision and recall remain acceptance targets until independently demonstrated. Neither the reference nor this research establishes reliable analysis of every possible tennis-match video.
